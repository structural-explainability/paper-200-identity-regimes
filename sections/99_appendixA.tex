\section{Pairwise Distinctions and Their Limits}
\label{app:sep}
% =============================================================================

The nine candidate profiles in Table~\ref{tab:master} yield
$\binom{9}{2}=36$ unordered pairs. The declared classification
functions are different for each pair, as established by
Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.DistinctCoreProfiles}.
This is a result about the \emph{proposed matrix}, not a proof
that all pairs induce distinct identity relations or represent
scientifically necessary identity criteria.

\subsection{Family-Level Generated Relations}

Definition~\ref{se200.def.InducedIdentityRelation} generates
$\sim_\tau$ using admissible steps of families classified
\PRS{} or \NEU{}.

This construction selects steps by their stipulated family
classifications rather than independently testing invariant
preservation at the individual-step level.

Within the stipulated matrix, $\OBL$ and $\REC$ have identical
sets of non-breaking families.
Consequently, on a common eligible domain with the same
admissible transformation dynamics, this paper's construction
generates equal relations for those profiles.
This is a formal comparison; the distinct intended carrier
kinds have not been shown to share a scientifically warranted
comparison domain.

The companion Persistence classification construction differs:
it generates relations using only families licensed \PRS{}.

Within the stipulated matrix, $\OBL/\RULEC$ and
$\SCOPES/\RULES$ have identical sets of \PRS{} families.
They therefore generate identical relations under that
construction on shared transformation dynamics.

Direct enumeration of the stipulated matrix gives seven
distinct \PRS{}-only family sets and eight distinct
\PRS{}-or-\NEU{} family sets.

These counts bound the number of distinct relations generated
on common dynamics by the respective classification-based
constructions.
They do not count scientifically required regimes and do not
bound the invariant-based step relations in general.

The remaining 34 unordered pairs have different sets of
\PRS{} families and can be separated under suitable dynamics.
Such differences do not prove separation on arbitrary dynamics,
which may lack steps witnessing those differences.

These \PRS-only results do not establish corresponding
equality or separation results for this paper's
\PRS{}-or-\NEU{} generated relation.

\subsection{Invariant-Generated Relations}

The newer invariant-based construction includes the admissible
individual steps that preserve a declared invariant, rather than
selecting entire families solely by their matrix labels.

Theorem~\ref{se200.thm.StepIdentityCollapse} establishes that
two bases on a common effect model and comparison domain
generate equal relations exactly when they agree on invariant
preservation for every admissible step in that domain.

Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.StepSeparation} establishes that
an admissible step preserved by one basis and changed by another
is a sufficient witness of different generated relations,
provided both endpoints lie in the comparison domain.

Conversely, bases that classify a family differently can still
produce equal relations on a restricted domain containing no
separating step.

None of these three generated-relation constructions establishes
strict identity without an independently justified interpretation.

\subsection{Conditional Examples}

The scope, rule, and fork models discussed in
Section~\ref{subsec:model_derived_results} demonstrate how
explicit presentation fields, transformation effects, and
warrants determine derived verdicts.
The fork model is especially instructive: a bare fork does
not entail either a change of locus or a change of object.
The proposed $\LOC/\OBJ$ distinction therefore requires
additional continuity warrants.
Whether the resulting generated relations coincide or differ
also depends on the admissible steps and comparison domain.

The stipulated matrix establishes pairwise differences among
the proposed classification profiles.
These differences do not necessarily imply distinct generated
relations.
The invariant-based step results provide explicit conditions
for relation equality and separation, while their interpretation
as strict identity requires independent scientific justification.
