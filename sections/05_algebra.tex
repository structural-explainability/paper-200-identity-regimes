\section{Transformation-Invariant Identity}
\label{sec:algebra}
% =============================================================================

The selected reference inventory of Section~\ref{sec:inventory} identifies
candidate carriers for comparing identity judgments under transformation.
This section examines how declared identity bases and classification profiles
can differ over the transformation basis of
Section~\ref{sec:formal_setting}.
A classification records proposed behavior relative to a specified basis;
it does not independently establish that two presentations have the same
real-world referent.
The scientific adequacy of the basis, the operation,
and their interpretation remains a separate obligation.

The inquiry draws on the distinction between reference kinds and identity
conditions familiar in formal ontology~\citep{guarino1998roles,guarinowelty2002}.
A kind name alone does not settle whether a transformation preserves its
intended bearer.
A stated identity basis helps formulate a test, but a
warranted persistence judgment also requires evidence and an appropriate
criterion for that referent.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Identity Bases}
\label{subsec:identity_bases}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Identity basis]
  \label{se200.def.IdentityBasis}
  An \emph{identity basis} is a stated criterion proposed for deciding when
  eligible presentations concern the same intended referent.
  It specifies
  the conditions whose preservation would warrant sameness under the
  interpretation in question.
  Its declaration alone does not establish
  that the criterion is adequate for the intended referent.
\end{definition}

The selected construction considers three candidate bases associated with
obligation-bearers, occurrences, and records, and three pairs of bases:
content versus structure for rules, extension versus structure for scopes,
and locus versus object for plain referents.
These are proposed criteria
for examining transformation behavior, not an exhaustive inventory of
scientifically adequate identity conditions.

A transformation classification is meaningful only relative to the
identity basis, the eligible comparison domain, and the actual effects
of the operation.
Different candidate bases may classify a selected
operation differently; that does not by itself decide which basis is
appropriate or whether the resulting judgments warrant strict identity.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Profiles and Collapse}
\label{subsec:profiles_and_collapse}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section~\ref{sec:formal_setting} defines identity regimes as proposed
basis--classification profiles and defines an equivalence relation
$\sim_\tau$ generated from specified non-breaking steps.
The generated relation and an independently warranted strict-identity
relation must not be conflated.

\begin{definition}[Collapse]
  \label{se200.def.Collapse}
  Fix a common comparison domain $D$ of eligible presentations for
  two profiles $\tau$ and $\tau'$.
  The profiles \emph{collapse at the level
    of generated relations on $D$} when the restrictions of their generated
  relations agree:
  \[
    {\sim_\tau}\big|_D = {\sim_{\tau'}}\big|_D.
  \]
  Those generated relations are \emph{distinct on $D$} if one relates
  an eligible pair that the other does not.
\end{definition}

Distinct classification functions do not necessarily induce distinct
generated relations.
For example, a family assigned \PRS{} under one profile and
\NEU{} under another preserves both bases on every step,
so the two profiles agree on that family's steps in the step-level construction.
In the older family-generated construction, which uses only families
classified \PRS{}, distinct sets of preserving families need not yield
distinct relations on arbitrary transformation dynamics,
because the distinguishing families may have no relevant steps.
Two pairs of profiles have identical preserving sets and so generate
identical family-level relations on every shared dynamics
(Appendix~\ref{app:sep}).

A stronger result holds in the invariant-based step construction:
when an admissible step inside a common comparison domain preserves
one basis invariant but changes another, the two step-generated
relations differ.
The endpoints of the separating step cannot be connected by any path of
steps that each preserve the second basis, because equality of invariant
values is preserved along such a path
(Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.StepSeparation}).
This is a mathematical fact about the specified invariants and admissible steps,
not independent scientific warrant for strict identity of the intended referents.

\begin{definition}[Underdetermined kind]
  \label{se200.def.UnderdeterminedKind}
  A reference kind is \emph{underdetermined at a transformation family}
  $f$ within the selected construction when two proposed bases for that
  kind assign, on a shared eligible comparison domain, a non-breaking
  classification and a breaking classification, respectively, to $f$.
\end{definition}

This is a difference between candidate classification profiles.
If both bases are adequately justified for a particular use, a system must
retain the distinction between the judgments they authorize.
It does not follow that they require separately implemented or
non-amalgamable relations: an explicit basis parameter may preserve both.
Nor does a classification difference alone establish a separating pair
in the generated equivalence relations or in the world.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Carrier Kinds Not Split in the Core Construction}
\label{subsec:core_unsplit_kinds}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected catalog specifies one candidate basis for each of three
kinds: obligation-bearer, occurrence, and record.
This selection does not establish that no other adequate bases exist.

For an \emph{obligation-bearer}, a possible criterion tracks the continuing
party rather than each obligation attributed to that party.
A change in location, assigned role, or obligation may preserve that party, provided
an appropriate account of the party's continuity warrants the judgment.
Replacement of the party may instead break its identity.
Organizational continuity and continuity of role occupancy must not be conflated.

For an \emph{occurrence}, temporal realization and provenance may supply
useful evidence, but they do not by themselves establish one bounded
whole.
Redescription of an independently individuated occurrence may
preserve it; treating a proper phase as the whole may not.
Decomposition, branching, and other transformations
cannot be classified uniformly without specifying
the occurrence and eligible presentations.

For a \emph{record}, content identity, token identity, version identity,
and continuity of custody can produce different judgments.
Copying a report may preserve its content while creating another token;
annotation may preserve an informational core while creating a new version.
Neither preservation nor breakage follows from the label ``record'' alone.

The selected profile names are
\[
  \OBL,\qquad \OCC,\qquad \REC.
\]
Their intended identity criteria and transformation classifications
remain subject to the qualifications above.

\subsection{Witnessed Split Kinds}
\label{subsec:witnessed_split_kinds}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The other three carrier kinds in the selected catalog have pairs of
candidate bases.
Their comparisons can be expressed by a common
classification pattern.
In particular, a difference between proposed
values of $\Class_\tau(f)$ is an algebraic fact about the profiles;
a difference between warranted identity relations requires more.

\begin{lemma}[Split pattern for classification profiles]
  \label{se200.lem.SplitPattern}
  Let $K$ be a reference kind and let $\tau_1,\tau_2$ be two candidate
  profiles for $K$ over the same transformation basis and a shared
  eligible comparison domain.
  If some $f\in\Fset$ satisfies
  \[
    \Class_{\tau_1}(f)\in\{\PRS,\NEU\}
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \Class_{\tau_2}(f)=\BRK,
  \]
  then the classification functions of $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$
  are distinct.
  This conclusion alone does not establish distinct generated
  equivalence relations, scientifically warranted strict-identity
  relations, or a minimum number of irreducible regimes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  The first classification is different from $\BRK$, so the
  classification functions disagree at $f$ and cannot be equal.
  Their generated relations depend on the eligible step pairs and
  equivalence closure; those relations, and their interpretations,
  are not determined by this disagreement alone.
\end{proof}

A stronger, referent-level separation would need a common eligible
pair of presentations together with independently justified identity
criteria that agree and disagree on that pair.
The following examples
show how candidate separating situations can be constructed.
They do
not prove that the corresponding criteria are necessary for every
realization of the reference kind.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Scope Under Decomposition}
\label{subsec:scope_split}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a scope presentation, distinguish the cases covered from their
internal organization.
Consider a finite set $C=\{a,b\}$ and two
partitions
\[
  P=\{\{a,b\}\},\qquad P'=\{\{a\},\{b\}\}.
\]
The presentations $(C,P)$ and $(C,P')$ have the same extension but
different partition structures.
A stipulated extension-fixed basis
identifies them; a stipulated structure-fixed basis distinguishes them.
An eligible decomposition that replaces $P$ by $P'$ while preserving $C$
therefore provides a candidate separating example.

\begin{proposition}[Scope underdetermination in the selected model]
  \label{se200.prop.ScopeUnderdetermination}
  If an eligible $\mathrm{AD}$ operation preserves the coverage $C$
  while changing the identity-relevant partition $P$, then the
  extension-fixed and structure-fixed candidate profiles can assign
  different classifications to that operation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Under the stipulated extension-fixed criterion, equality of $C$
  supplies preservation; under the stipulated structure-fixed
  criterion, inequality of $P$ supplies breakage.
  On a domain where these are the specified effects of $\mathrm{AD}$,
  its two classifications differ, as in
  Lemma~\ref{se200.lem.SplitPattern}.
\end{proof}

We call the two proposed profiles
\[
  \SCOPEE,\qquad \SCOPES.
\]
This example demonstrates a distinction between coverage and
structure within the model.
It does not show that partition equality
is the correct identity criterion for every scope, or that a scope
cannot be faithfully represented with an explicit basis parameter.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Rule Under Refinement}
\label{subsec:rule_split}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a rule presentation, distinguish the specified normative content
from its textual or organizational structure.
Consider two
presentations $(N,S)$ and $(N,S')$ with identical stipulated content
$N$ but different structures $S\ne S'$.
A content-fixed comparison
identifies the presentations, whereas a structure-fixed comparison
separates them.
Whether a particular refinement actually preserves
normative content must be assessed rather than inferred from the word
``refinement.''

\begin{proposition}[Rule underdetermination in the selected model]
  \label{se200.prop.RuleUnderdetermination}
  If an eligible $\mathrm{RF}$ operation preserves the specified
  content $N$ while changing the identity-relevant structure $S$,
  then the content-fixed and structure-fixed candidate profiles can
  assign different classifications to that operation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The stipulated content-fixed criterion is preserved because $N$
  is unchanged; the stipulated structure-fixed criterion is broken
  because $S\ne S'$.
  The corresponding classifications differ,
  as in Lemma~\ref{se200.lem.SplitPattern}.
\end{proof}

We call the two proposed profiles
\[
  \RULEC,\qquad \RULES.
\]
This comparison does not decide whether a particular statute or rule
persists across amendment, recodification, or re-expression.
That
requires a justified account of the relevant rule referent.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Plain Referent Under Forking}
\label{subsec:plain_referent_split}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

For plain referents, distinguish a locus from an object associated
with that locus.
A toy configuration $(L,O)$ records both.
Two
configurations $(L,O_1)$ and $(L,O_2)$ may have the same locus $L$
but different objects $O_1\ne O_2$.
A locus-fixed comparison then
identifies them, while an object-fixed comparison distinguishes them.
The inequality of the objects is an assumption of the toy model,
not a conclusion from their labels alone.

\begin{proposition}[Plain-referent underdetermination in the selected model]
  \label{se200.prop.PlainReferentUnderdetermination}
  If an eligible $\mathrm{BF}$ operation yields compared presentations
  with the same locus but independently distinct objects, then
  locus-fixed and object-fixed candidate profiles can assign
  different classifications to that comparison.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The locus-fixed criterion is preserved by the shared locus; the
  object-fixed criterion is broken by the stipulated object
  distinction.
  The candidate classifications consequently differ,
  as in Lemma~\ref{se200.lem.SplitPattern}.
\end{proof}

We call the two proposed profiles
\[
  \LOC,\qquad \OBJ.
\]
A fork of record representations does not by itself create distinct
objects or imply loss of object identity.
Nor does relocation of an
object necessarily end its identity.
The separating case depends on
which referent is being tracked and whether the assumed operation
and object distinction are independently warranted.

\subsection{Result of the Algebra}
\label{subsec:algebra_result}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected inventory contributes three single-basis candidate
profiles and three pairs of candidate profiles:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
     & \OBL,\quad \OCC,\quad \REC,                       \\
     & \LOC,\quad \OBJ,\quad \SCOPEE,\quad \SCOPES,\quad
    \RULEC,\quad \RULES.
  \end{aligned}
\]
These are nine named carrier--basis profiles in the chosen catalog.
The conditional examples show how different identity bases can
classify specified transformations differently.
They do not establish
that every transformation of the named family has the same effect,
that the generated relations warrant strict identity, or that all
nine profiles are scientifically necessary or non-amalgamable.

Section~\ref{sec:nine} collects the proposed classifications;
Section~\ref{sec:distinctions} distinguishes the catalog count from an
unproved lower bound on irreducible identity regimes.
