\section{Formal Setting}
\label{sec:formal_setting}
% =============================================================================

This paper investigates candidate conditions for stable reference under
transformation in settings of persistent interpretive disagreement.
Its formal results are relative to the selected carriers, identity bases,
transformation families, and assumptions stated below.


% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Persistent Disagreement}
\label{subsec:persistent_disagreement}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

We consider environments in which interpretive frameworks may remain
in disagreement and their extensions need not be coordinated.
Frameworks may advance incompatible causal accounts, normative attributions,
or interpretive classifications without being required to converge.
The neutrality constraint of Section~\ref{sec:background} concerns which
propositions the shared substrate itself adopts; it does not adjudicate
among those interpretations.

This setting assumes neither that all admissible frameworks agree nor that
they are mutually consistent.
The conditional neutrality result of
\emph{Neutral Substrates} also requires Referential Common Ground: the
complete adopted referential commitment set must remain jointly compatible
with each admissible framework.
That premise is not established by the
present inventory or transformation classifications.

Revising a substrate merely to make conflicting interpretations converge
would not resolve their disagreement neutrally.
Revising an inadequately
justified referential commitment is a different matter: any revised
construction requires renewed examination of its own compatibility and
referential warrant.
This paper does not define such a revision procedure.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Stable Reference in Strong Form}
\label{subsec:stable_reference_strong}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

We examine the demanding case in which representations of a purported
referent change through ordinary record transformations while the question
of its identity remains meaningful.
The referential regime in
\emph{Neutral Substrates} supplies abstract individuation, co-reference,
and persistence interfaces; it does not independently determine which
referents persist through a particular operation.

Stable reference requires identifying the intended referent and supplying
an adequate criterion for when presentations concern that same referent.
A change in location, role, description, or state need not constitute a
change of bearer identity; conversely, preservation of a record or label
does not establish the persistence of what it represents.
The applicable judgment depends on the proposed identity criterion and
its independent justification.

Roles, flags, contextual predicates, and carrier/basis parameters are not
inherently incompatible with neutral shared reference.
They may carry
explicit, fixed distinctions.
What matters is whether their meanings and
use are adequately specified and whether the resulting referential
commitments satisfy the relevant common-ground conditions.
A convention whose identity-relevant meaning
is left to conflicting interpretations
cannot, without further grounds, establish a shared identity judgment.

\subsection{Transformation Basis}
\label{subsec:transformation_basis}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Identity questions become visible when representations are transformed.
We select the following finite transformation basis for analysis:
\[
  \Fset =
  \{\mathrm{RE},\mathrm{AN},\mathrm{RF},\mathrm{AD},\mathrm{RC},
  \mathrm{RA},\mathrm{SU},\mathrm{BF},\mathrm{PV},\mathrm{SE}\}.
\]
The basis is a selected set of operation families, not a claim of
completeness for record systems or of identity effects uniform across
all referent kinds.

\begin{definition}[Transformation basis]
  \label{se200.def.TransformationBasis}
  The transformation basis $\Fset$ consists of these operation families:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
    \item $\mathrm{RE}$, re-expression: change of representation format while preserving represented content;
    \item $\mathrm{AN}$, annotation: addition of non-identity-bearing information;
    \item $\mathrm{RF}$, refinement: increase in descriptive, structural, or normative detail;
    \item $\mathrm{AD}$, aggregation or decomposition: restructuring into coarser or finer components under a specified whole-preservation condition;
    \item $\mathrm{RC}$, re-contextualization: change in applicability context;
    \item $\mathrm{RA}$, reassignment: change in association, bearer, or assignment relation;
    \item $\mathrm{SU}$, substitution: replacement of the designated carrier;
    \item $\mathrm{BF}$, branch or fork: divergence into multiple continuations;
    \item $\mathrm{PV}$, provenance extension: addition of historical, derivational, or evidentiary metadata;
    \item $\mathrm{SE}$, state evolution: change in state of an enduring referent over time.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

These names designate typed operation families.
Their meaning and
identity effects must be evaluated relative to an intended referent,
eligible presentations, and a stated identity basis.
In particular,
$\mathrm{AD}$ may preserve the covered cases of a scope while changing
its internal organization; whether either property is identity-relevant
is a separate question.
Changes to covered cases are not represented
by that particular whole-preserving instance of $\mathrm{AD}$.

If a larger transformation basis preserves the previously assigned
classifications on $\Fset$, then any existing classification disagreement
remains a disagreement on the larger basis, as expressed in
Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.ClassificationDistinctionPersists}.

\subsection{Classification of Transformations}
\label{subsec:classification_transformations}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The construction assigns a classification to each operation family
relative to a declared identity basis and its intended comparison domain.

\begin{definition}[Transformation classification]
  \label{se200.def.TransformationClassification}
  For a profile $\tau$ and transformation family $f\in\Fset$,
  \[
    \Class_\tau(f)\in\{\PRS,\BRK,\NEU,\NA\}.
  \]
  The proposed classifications mean:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
    \item $\PRS$: the operation preserves the declared identity basis;
    \item $\BRK$: the operation breaks the declared identity basis;
    \item $\NEU$: the operation is applicable but does not affect that basis;
    \item $\NA$: the operation is inapplicable on the selected domain.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The four-valued classification above describes the stipulated
profile catalog.
The canonical Lean matrix uses
three classification values (IGN, PRS, BRK),
mapping both \NEU{} and N/A to IGN.
The companion Persistence classification restores applicability
at the N/A cells of Table~\ref{tab:master}.

The newer invariant-based construction instead derives one of
five verdicts:
N/A, BRK, MIX, PRS, or NEU.
It also distinguishes
a modeled N/A verdict from a matrix cell for which no derivation
has been supplied.

These representations are not interchangeable without an
explicit semantic correspondence.

$\PRS$ and $\NEU$ both designate non-breaking behavior in this
classification scheme, but they express different reasons.
A family-level value is meaningful only when its proposed behavior
is sufficiently uniform over the specified eligible operations;
otherwise the intended scope must be refined.
Neither the classification label nor its recorded presence in a
profile independently warrants strict identity for particular referents.

\begin{definition}[Non-breaking transformation]
  \label{se200.def.NonBreakingTransformation}
  A family $f$ is \emph{non-breaking} for profile $\tau$ when
  \[
    \Class_\tau(f)\in\{\PRS,\NEU\}.
  \]
  It is \emph{breaking} when $\Class_\tau(f)=\BRK$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Identity Regimes and Generated Relations}
\label{subsec:identity_regimes}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

An identity regime is a proposed pairing of an identity basis with a
transformation classification.
It is not itself an ontological kind
or proof that its basis is adequate for a particular referent.

\begin{definition}[Identity regime]
  \label{se200.def.IdentityRegime}
  An \emph{identity regime} $\tau$ consists of a declared identity basis
  and a classification function
  \[
    \Class_\tau:\Fset\to\{\PRS,\BRK,\NEU,\NA\}.
  \]
  The basis proposes conditions of sameness for the intended referent;
  the classification records proposed transformation behavior relative
  to that basis.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Induced relation from non-breaking families]
  \label{se200.def.InducedIdentityRelation}
  Fix a profile $\tau$ and a domain $D_\tau$ of eligible presentations.
  Let $E_\tau\subseteq D_\tau\times D_\tau$ consist of pairs joined
  by specified, admissible steps of transformation families $f$
  satisfying
  \[
    \Class_\tau(f)\in\{\PRS,\NEU\}.
  \]
  Define $\sim_\tau$ as the smallest equivalence relation
  on $D_\tau$ containing $E_\tau$.
\end{definition}

This is a classification-based family-level construction.
It selects admissible steps according to the stipulated family
classifications; it does not independently establish that each
step preserves the declared identity basis.

The companion Persistence construction defines a different
family-level relation, generated only by steps of families
classified \PRS{}.
Under common domains and transformation dynamics, the
\PRS{}-only generated relation is contained in the relation
generated by \PRS{} and \NEU{} families.

The newer invariant-based construction instead evaluates
preservation at the level of individual admissible steps.
Its generating edges are exactly the steps that preserve
the declared invariant, including preserving steps of
families whose derived verdict is \MIX{}.
The three constructions must not be conflated.

Each generated relation is an equivalence relation by construction.
None independently establishes strict identity of the intended
real-world referents.
Such an interpretation requires an independently justified
identity criterion, evidence supporting the admissible steps,
and justification that equivalence closure preserves the criterion.

In particular, branching or a chain of classified non-breaking
steps cannot, by itself, discharge these scientific obligations.

\subsection{Basis Plurality}
\label{subsec:basis_plurality}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

A carrier kind may admit more than one proposed identity basis.
For example, one can compare locus-fixed with object-fixed criteria,
coverage with structural organization, or rule content with rule structure.
These comparisons are well defined whichever basis an application adopts.

\begin{definition}[Basis plurality]
  \label{se200.def.BasisPlurality}
  A carrier kind exhibits \emph{basis plurality} within a construction
  when at least two distinct identity bases are specified for it.
  Whether both bases suit a given application is a separate question.
\end{definition}

The selected construction examines pairs of bases for plain referents
(locus or object), scopes (extension or structure), and rules
(content or structure).

\subsection{Hidden Regimes}
\label{subsec:hidden_regimes}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

A representation can fail to disclose an identity-relevant distinction
on which its identity judgments nevertheless depend.
This issue is
separate from whether those judgments are expressed in one relation
or several relations.

\begin{definition}[Hidden regime]
  \label{se200.def.HiddenRegime}
  A \emph{hidden regime} is an identity-relevant criterion or distinction
  used to determine sameness in a realization but not adequately
  declared or fixed in the shared referential account, so that its
  application depends on an unstated or contested interpretation.
\end{definition}

A declared role, flag, carrier tag, or basis parameter is not hidden
merely because it is a discriminator.
Its adequacy depends on whether
its semantics are fixed and its use is justified for the intended
referents.
Conversely, an unacknowledged convention cannot be treated
as established common ground merely because a system implements it.
Whether a proposed combined representation is faithful must therefore
be examined rather than decided by terminology alone.

\subsection{Scope of the Analysis}
\label{subsec:scope_of_analysis}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected carrier inventory, identity bases, and transformation
classifications supply a finite candidate construction.
Sections~\ref{sec:algebra}--\ref{sec:distinctions} analyze its
transformation distinctions and the relations they generate.
The construction does not supply a complete inventory of identity
criteria or a general procedure for determining strict identity
under transformation.

The next section presents the selected reference inventory.
Its carrier
kinds identify subjects of inquiry; they do not themselves supply
independent identity warrants.
