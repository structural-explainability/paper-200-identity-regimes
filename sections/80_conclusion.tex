\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================

Naming a reference kind does not settle the identity of its referents.
Nor does classifying a transformation as preserving or breaking identity,
by itself, establish a warranted judgment of sameness.
Such judgments require independently justified individuation,
co-reference, and persistence criteria for the intended referents.
Transformation analysis can make proposed criteria and their consequences
explicit, but it cannot supply their scientific justification by stipulation.

This paper takes its motivation from the conditional neutrality result in
\emph{Neutral Substrates}~\citep{case2026neutral}.
Under Referential Common Ground and the stated restriction on
foundational commitments, that result provides sufficient conditions
for semantic neutrality.
It does not establish the identity criteria
required for particular referents.
The present paper examines selected
candidate criteria and how they classify transformations of representations.

The selected reference inventory and candidate carrier--basis
profiles are presented in Section~\ref{sec:profiles}.

Three paired comparisons motivate closer investigation:
coverage versus internal structure for scopes,
content versus structure for rules,
and locus versus object for plain referents.
Decomposition, refinement, and forking supply conditional tests of
these proposed distinctions, not universal identity laws.

Within the selected finite construction, the declared classification
functions are pairwise distinct, the specified carrier--basis assignment
is determinate, and classification differences persist when the
transformation basis is extended without changing existing assignments.
These results concern declared profiles and their formal properties.
A difference between classifications does not necessarily produce
a difference between generated equivalence relations, and neither
establishes strict identity or non-identity without further warrant.

The carrier-indexed combination
considered in Section~\ref{sec:distinctions} shows why distinguishable
relations need not be counted as separate formal structures.
Whether a proposed combination faithfully preserves all warranted
identity judgments and transformation distinctions remains a
substantive question, as does any proposed non-amalgamation condition.

The remaining work is therefore both mathematical and scientific:
justify the proposed identity bases for their intended referents;
validate transformation classifications on eligible presentations;
distinguish generated relations from warranted co-reference and
persistence judgments; and determine what, if anything, requires
irreducibly separate representations.
The application settings in
Section~\ref{sec:application} offer contexts in which to test these
conditions, rather than evidence that the selected catalog is complete.

The contribution of this paper is a conditional formal
method for deriving and auditing identity-preservation
classifications under transformation.

The certified model-derived cells demonstrate how explicitly
declared identity invariants, transformation effects, and
additional warrants determine classification verdicts.
The step-generated relation results establish precise
conditions for equality and separation of the resulting
formal identity relations.

These results make proposed identity claims more auditable,
provide testable conditions for transforming systems, and
identify assumptions that must be independently justified.

The remaining scientific questions concern the adequacy of
the identity criteria, the correspondence between formal
models and real transformations, and the compatibility of
the separately modeled classifications.

Companion operational-identity and accountable-records work
can examine agreement between implementations and declared criteria;
that agreement alone cannot certify the criteria's scientific adequacy.

Stable reference under persistent disagreement remains the objective.
