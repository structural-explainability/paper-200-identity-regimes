\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================

This paper pairs candidate identity bases with families of record
transformations and derives, rather than stipulates, how selected
families classify relative to a basis.
Invariant-defined bases and explicit effect models yield verdicts,
certified in Lean~4 for scope decomposition, rule refinement, and
forking, that expose their dependence on field granularity, admissible
steps, and continuity warrants.

The identity relation generated by invariant-preserving steps has an
exact characterization: two bases generate equal relations on a
comparison domain precisely when they agree on the preservation of
every admissible step there, and a single separating step witnesses a
difference.
Under stated assumptions, classification-based relations can be
compared with the step-level construction.
The carrier-indexed combination preserves relations on distinct
carrier kinds within one tagged relation.

Coverage versus internal structure for scopes, content versus
structure for rules, and locus versus object for plain referents serve
as conditional tests of the proposed distinctions, with decomposition,
refinement, and forking as the testing operations.

These results make identity claims about transforming records
auditable, supply testable conditions for systems that transform them,
and identify the assumptions behind each verdict.
They are conditional on the declared bases, effect models, and
warrants.
Naming a reference kind or classifying a transformation does not
settle the identity of its referents, so applying the results to
particular referents requires independently justified individuation,
co-reference, and persistence criteria.

The paper takes its motivation from the conditional neutrality result of
\emph{Neutral Substrates}~\citep{case2026neutral}, which gives
sufficient conditions for semantic neutrality under Referential Common
Ground but does not supply identity criteria for particular referents.

Remaining work is mathematical and scientific: justify the proposed
identity bases for their intended referents, validate transformation
classifications on eligible presentations, relate generated relations
to warranted co-reference and persistence judgments, and determine
which distinctions require separate representation.
The application settings of Section~\ref{sec:application} offer
contexts for these tests, and companion operational-identity and
accountable-records work can examine agreement between implementations
and declared criteria.

Stable reference under persistent disagreement remains the objective.
