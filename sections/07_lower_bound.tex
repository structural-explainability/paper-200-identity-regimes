\section{Regime Distinctions and the Limits of Counting}
\label{sec:bound}
% =============================================================================

The preceding sections specify a finite inventory of carrier kinds,
proposed identity bases, and transformation classifications.
They exhibit distinctions within that construction.
A separate question is whether every realization preserving those
distinctions must implement a particular minimum number of
irreducible identity regimes.
The present construction does not establish such a lower bound.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Inventory and Its Identity Conditions}
\label{subsec:inventory_floor}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section~\ref{sec:inventory} selects six carrier kinds for the
accountability setting considered here:
obligation-bearers, rules, occurrences, scopes, records, and plain referents.
To reason about stable reference to any of these carriers, a realization
needs adequate criteria for the relevant referents and the identity
judgments it makes about them.
The selection of these six kinds, however, is an assumption of the
chosen inventory, not a proof that all neutral substrates require them.

Nor does the distinction between carrier kinds by itself establish a
minimum number of irreducible identity regimes.
Distinct representation domains can have distinguishable identity
relations without those relations requiring separate, non-amalgamable
formal structures.
A lower-bound claim must first say what counts as one regime and which
ways of combining relations are admissible.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Witnessed Differences Within the Selected Profiles}
\label{subsec:witnessed_splits}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proposed core contains three pairs of identity bases associated
with the same carrier kind.
These pairs motivate tests of whether ordinary transformations make
different identity judgments under the respective bases.

For plain referents, the proposed locus-fixed and object-fixed bases
are compared using branch/fork operations
(Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.PlainReferentUnderdetermination}).
A particular fork may preserve a locus while distinguishing the objects
associated with that locus.
The question for a realization is which bearer each basis tracks and
whether the proposed preservation and breakage judgments are warranted.

For scopes, the proposed extension-fixed and structure-fixed bases
are compared using aggregation and decomposition
(Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.ScopeUnderdetermination}).
An operation may preserve coverage while changing component structure.
Whether that difference is an identity difference depends on the
independently justified criterion for the scope in question.

For rules, the proposed content-fixed and structure-fixed bases
are compared using refinement
(Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.RuleUnderdetermination}).
A refinement may preserve specified content while changing structure.
Again, the relevant sameness judgment depends on what the rule is
and on which criterion of its identity is justified.

If two justified identity relations disagree on the same eligible pair
of presentations, they are distinct relations on that comparison domain.
A difference between declared transformation classifications alone,
however, does not establish such a disagreement about real referents.
It requires a sound interpretation of the operation, its applicable
presentations, and the asserted identity basis.
The three pairs are therefore useful candidate distinctions, not
independent demonstrations of universally required regimes.

\subsection{What the Finite Catalog Counts}
\label{subsec:the_bound}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The catalog of Section~\ref{sec:nine} contains nine named
carrier--basis profiles:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\OBL,\quad \OCC,\quad \REC,\quad \LOC,\quad \OBJ,\\
\SCOPEE,\quad \SCOPES,\quad \RULEC,\quad \RULES .
\end{gathered}
\]
Its cardinality is the arithmetic of the selected construction:
\[
1 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 9.
\]
The count records three selected single-basis carriers and three
selected two-basis carriers.
It is not, by itself, a minimum number of independent identity
relations, a lower bound on every faithful realization, or an
exhaustive inventory of admissible identity criteria.

Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.DistinctCoreProfiles} concerns distinctions
among the specified profiles.
Even when profile distinctions are formally established, the further
claim that they cannot be represented together without loss is a
separate obligation.

\subsection{Carrier-Indexed Combination and the Counting Question}
\label{subsec:amalgamation}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

An elementary construction illustrates why distinct carrier kinds do
not, without more, establish an irreducible regime count.
Let $K$ index the selected carrier kinds, and let $D_k$ be a domain
of presentations for carrier kind $k$.
Suppose $E_k$ is an equivalence relation on $D_k$ for each $k\in K$.
Use the tagged disjoint union
\[
D = \bigsqcup_{k\in K} D_k
\]
and define a relation $E$ on $D$ by
\[
(k,x)\mathrel{E}(\ell,y)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k=\ell\ \text{and}\ x\mathrel{E_k}y.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Carrier-indexed combination]
\label{se200.prop.CarrierIndexedCombination}
Then $E$ is an equivalence relation on $D$.
Restricting $E$ to the $k$-th summand recovers $E_k$
for each $k\in K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Reflexivity and symmetry follow from the corresponding properties
of each $E_k$.
If $(k,x)\mathrel{E}(\ell,y)$ and
$(\ell,y)\mathrel{E}(m,z)$, then $k=\ell=m$ and
$x\mathrel{E_k}y\mathrel{E_k}z$.
Transitivity of $E_k$ therefore gives $(k,x)\mathrel{E}(m,z)$.
For presentations in the $k$-th summand, the definition of $E$
is exactly the definition of $E_k$.
\end{proof}

This construction preserves the component equivalence relations
without identifying presentations belonging to different carrier kinds.
It therefore blocks an inference from disjoint carrier domains alone
to an irreducible count of identity relations.
It does not prove that the combined relation is one scientifically
adequate identity regime, that it preserves all required transformation
semantics, or that it satisfies the Neutral Substrate constraint.
In particular, it does not resolve the differing identity bases
proposed for the same carrier kind.

The unresolved question is whether a faithful realization can combine
the selected identity criteria while retaining every warranted
identity judgment and transformation distinction.
A publicly declared carrier or basis parameter is not automatically
a hidden regime merely because it is a parameter.
Conversely, naming a parameter does not establish that its semantics
are adequate or that it preserves the required distinctions.
The admissibility of such a combination, and any scientifically
justified non-amalgamation condition, require further analysis.
The lossless-union comparison (CE-5) is a specific test of this issue.

\subsection{Separation Under an Extended Transformation Basis}
\label{subsec:monotonicity}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

A narrower monotonicity result applies to the declared
classification profiles.
Let $\Fset\subseteq\Fset'$ be transformation bases.
Suppose the classifications of two profiles on $\Fset'$ agree
with their existing classifications on every operation in $\Fset$.

\begin{proposition}[Persistence of a classification distinction]
\label{se200.prop.ClassificationDistinctionPersists}
If two profiles have different classifications for some
$f\in\Fset$, their classification functions remain distinct
under extensions to $\Fset'$ that preserve the original classifications.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The extended classification functions still disagree at $f$,
so they cannot be equal.
\end{proof}

This result concerns the preservation of a previously recorded
classification difference.
It does not establish a minimum number of irreducible identity
regimes, nor does it establish that a classification difference
warrants strict identity or non-identity for particular referents.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Boundary of the Result}
\label{subsec:bound_not_claim}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The present analysis records a finite set of proposed carrier--basis
profiles, identifies conditional transformation-level distinctions,
and establishes limited formal consequences of the stated
classification and relation constructions.
The selected carrier inventory and candidate identity bases require
independent scientific justification for their intended referents.

In particular, this analysis does not establish that six carrier kinds
require six irreducible regimes, that the nine selected profiles impose
a universal nine-regime lower bound, or that a distinct classification
profile necessarily determines a distinct, warranted identity relation.
A necessity result would require, among other things, a justified unit
of counting, sound identity and transformation interpretations,
and an adequate account of admissible combinations and non-amalgamation.

These remain open scientific and mathematical questions.
The limitations on the broader application of the construction
are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.
