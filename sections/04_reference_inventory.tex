\section{Reference Inventory}
\label{sec:inventory}
% =============================================================================

Before analyzing identity under transformation, we select a finite
inventory of referents encountered in the accountability settings considered
in this paper.
These settings call for descriptions of parties, rules,
occurrences, scopes, records, and things acted upon or observed.
The inventory organizes questions of reference without adopting a
contested causal or normative interpretation as a substrate commitment.

The inventory is not a theorem, an upper ontology, or a complete domain
ontology.
It does not establish that all neutral substrates require these
six kinds, that the kinds are mutually exclusive, or that their names
supply adequate criteria of individuation or persistence.
Their suitability for particular referents requires independent examination.

Reference kinds organize the intended referents; identity bases propose
conditions for their sameness under transformation.
A distinction among
kinds or bases does not, by itself, establish a minimum number of
irreducible identity regimes.
Section~\ref{sec:distinctions} examines this
counting question separately.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Reference Kinds and Identity Carriers}
\label{subsec:reference_kinds_identity_carriers}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Reference kind]
  \label{se200.def.ReferenceKind}
  A \emph{reference kind} is a class of intended referents selected to
  organize questions of reference in the accountability settings under
  analysis.
  Membership in a reference kind does not itself determine
  a referent's unity, sameness, or persistence criteria.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Identity carrier]
  \label{se200.def.IdentityCarrier}
  An \emph{identity carrier} is an intended referent considered as the
  subject of a proposed sameness, difference, or persistence judgment
  under transformation.
  The designation does not itself warrant that
  judgment or determine the applicable identity basis.
\end{definition}

A reference kind can organize several proposed identity bases.
Different kinds may also support compatible criteria or share referents
considered in different capacities.
In either case, the adequacy of
an identity judgment depends on an identified bearer and an independently
justified criterion, not solely on a kind name or transformation label.

The selected inventory is:

\begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.68\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    Reference kind & Referent considered in this analysis \\
    \midrule
    Obligation-bearer &
    a party considered as a possible bearer of responsibility, duty,
    liability, or authority \\
    Rule &
    a rule or normative instrument considered separately from an act
    of enacting, interpreting, or applying it \\
    Occurrence &
    a happening such as a filing, inspection, transfer, or decision,
    whose unity and identity require specified criteria \\
    Scope &
    a domain of applicability described by places, parties, periods,
    procedures, classifications, or conditions \\
    Record &
    a document, entry, or other informational record that can be
    referred to independently of what it describes or asserts \\
    Plain referent &
    a site, object, facility, instrument, sample, or asset considered
    apart from any assigned obligation-bearing or rule-governing role \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{center}

These categories draw on distinctions familiar in foundational ontology,
legal ontology, provenance, and institutional recordkeeping.
No novelty or independent necessity is claimed for this inventory.
Its role is to provide candidate carriers for the subsequent analysis.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Obligation-Bearers}
\label{subsec:inventory_obligation_bearers}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected settings include claims about parties that may bear
responsibility, duty, liability, authority, or accountability.
The substrate can preserve reference to a party and record that a source
attributes an obligation to it without adopting that attribution as
an unqualified normative conclusion.

In multi-jurisdiction regulatory reporting, frameworks may identify
different entities or capacities as relevant: a parent organization,
subsidiary, facility, office, or delegated agent.
The organizational
entity, an institutional role, and the occupant of that role need not
have identical persistence criteria.
Whether two presentations concern
the same bearer requires an account appropriate to the intended referent.

Distinguishing a bearer from occurrences in which obligations are
assigned, contested, fulfilled, or discharged supports comparisons
across time.
One bearer may participate in several occurrences, and
one occurrence may involve several bearers.
These observations motivate
separate reference questions; they do not prove that the corresponding
identity relations cannot be represented in a common formal structure.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Rules}
\label{subsec:inventory_rules}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected settings include reference to rules and normative
instruments that may be enacted, amended, delegated, interpreted,
applied, challenged, or repealed.
Recording such a referent does not
require the substrate to declare that the rule is valid, binding,
justified, or correctly interpreted.

Legislative history analysis distinguishes questions about a statute
from questions about the enactment, amendment, agency action, or
judicial decision associated with it.
A statute may remain citable
across those occurrences, but citation continuity alone does not
settle whether successive texts, versions, or normative contents
constitute the same rule.

The proposed content-fixed and structure-fixed bases examine different
responses to refinement.
Whether either basis supplies a warranted
identity criterion depends on which rule referent is intended and
what the transformation actually preserves.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Occurrences}
\label{subsec:inventory_occurrences}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected settings include occurrences such as filings, inspections,
votes, transfers, transactions, observations, decisions, enactments,
amendments, and enforcement actions.
Their temporal realization and
provenance can help identify them but do not necessarily determine
where one occurrence begins or ends, which phases belong to one whole,
or when two reports concern the same happening.

For example, jurisdictions may disagree about whether a filing
constitutes compliance while referring to an occurrence they have
independently identified as the same filing.
Similarly, institutions
may dispute the significance of an enactment or a measurement without
thereby disputing its occurrence identity.
In other cases, that identity
itself may be contested and cannot simply be assumed as common ground.

An occurrence can be distinguished for analysis from its participants,
the records describing it, and the rules invoked in its evaluation.
Such analytic distinctions do not independently establish disjoint
ontological domains or irreducible identity regimes.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Scopes}
\label{subsec:inventory_scopes}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected settings include applicability domains described by
places, parties, periods, procedures, classifications, or conditions.
Scopes may be territorial, institutional, temporal, procedural,
classificatory, jurisdictional, or conditional.
Their boundaries
and relevance may themselves be disputed.

In multi-jurisdiction reporting, a rule may be associated with
different entities, facilities, transactions, or reporting periods.
Tracking a scope separately in an analysis can permit comparison of
coverage while holding the rule description fixed, or comparison of
rule versions while holding coverage fixed.
This is a useful modeling
choice, not proof that every substrate must make scope an independent
identity carrier.

The proposed extension-fixed and structure-fixed bases distinguish
coverage from internal organization.
A decomposition may preserve
covered cases while changing organization; whether it preserves
identity depends on the justified scope criterion, not solely on
the operation's name.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Records}
\label{subsec:inventory_records}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected settings include descriptive documents, dataset entries,
observation records, provenance items, and other informational records.
A substrate may record that a source asserted a claim without thereby
adopting its causal or normative content.

In cross-institutional provenance, a record may be produced, received,
annotated, copied, transferred, or reanalyzed.
The institutions may
disagree about what it shows.
Whether successive presentations are
of the same record nevertheless depends on the identity basis:
content, a particular token, a version, or a custody-linked object
may give different answers.

Distinguishing a record from what it describes permits separate
questions about an assertion and its subject.
It does not imply that
annotation, copying, or transfer always preserves record identity,
or that record identity is an irreducible regime.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Plain Referents}
\label{subsec:inventory_plain_referents}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected settings also include things acted upon, regulated,
located, transferred, observed, measured, or described: facilities,
parcels, instruments, devices, environmental sites, physical objects,
infrastructure components, samples, and assets.

The designation \emph{plain referent} indicates how a referent is being
considered in the present analysis, not a claim that the referent
could never bear an obligation, occupy a role, or appear in another
reference kind.
A facility may be inspected; a parcel may fall within
an applicability scope; an instrument may produce a measurement record;
and a sample may pass between institutions.
Whether the referent
persists through these operations remains a separate question.

The proposed locus-fixed and object-fixed bases distinguish questions
about a place from questions about an object associated with that
place.
A branch or fork may provide a useful test of their different
transformation responses, but does not by itself establish that they
are two identity criteria for one and the same kind of bearer.
The intended referents and eligible operations must be specified.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Summary}
\label{subsec:inventory_summary}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

This paper selects six reference kinds: obligation-bearer, rule,
occurrence, scope, record, and plain referent.
They organize a finite
set of candidate identity questions; they are neither a complete
ontology nor a proven minimal inventory for neutral substrates.

The distinction between a selected carrier and its proposed identity
basis remains essential.
The inventory does not determine a warranted
identity relation, prove the necessity of each candidate basis, or
establish the non-amalgamation of the resulting relations.

The next section compares proposed identity bases and transformation
behavior for these selected carriers under the formal setting of
Section~\ref{sec:formal_setting}.
