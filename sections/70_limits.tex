\section{Limits and Boundary Conditions}
\label{sec:limits}
% =============================================================================

The results of this paper concern a selected finite construction:
six reference kinds, nine candidate carrier--basis profiles, and
classifications over a stated transformation basis.
Within that construction, the paper establishes specified
classification-profile distinctions and the determinacy of a stipulated
profile assignment.
It does not establish a universal lower bound on
irreducible identity regimes or certify that the selected bases correctly
characterize identity for real-world referents.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Conditional Scope}
\label{subsec:limits_conditional_scope}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The analysis is motivated by the neutral-substrate constraint of
\emph{Neutral Substrates}~\citep{case2026neutral} and by the setting of
persistent interpretive disagreement described in
Section~\ref{sec:formal_setting}.
The neutrality result in that earlier work is conditional on Referential
Common Ground and independently justified referential commitments.
Neither the inventory selected here nor its transformation classifications
establishes those premises for a concrete system.

The present results are relative to the selected carrier kinds,
candidate identity bases, eligible transformation instances, and
declared classification assignments.
Their mathematical validity within the specified construction must be
distinguished from the scientific adequacy of those choices.
In particular, a record's stable label, a declared basis, or a
non-breaking transformation classification does not, by itself,
warrant strict identity across presentations.

The six certified classification cells establish consequences
of explicit invariant bases, effect models, and warrants.
They do not establish that those models adequately describe
the transformations or identity criteria of an actual system.

Individual derived cells may rely on different presentation
spaces, field decompositions, and admissibility conditions.
Their separate certification does not establish a coherent
common semantic realization of the entire classification matrix.

Empirical counterexamples can challenge a proposed model or
universal verdict.
Finite observations alone do not establish universal conformance
of an unrestricted implementation to that model.

The analysis is not tied to a particular legal theory, policy framework,
or causal account.
That independence of vocabulary does not prove that
its selected identity conditions are valid in every such domain.

\subsection{No Lower Bound, Upper Bound, or Completeness Claim}
\label{subsec:limits_no_completeness}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The catalog in Section~\ref{sec:nine} contains nine named
carrier--basis profiles.
The number nine counts the selected
construction; it is not a proven minimum number of distinct identity
relations, irreducible regimes, or structures required in every
faithful realization.
Nor is it an upper bound or a complete inventory
of possible identity bases and referent kinds.

Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.DistinctCoreProfiles} establishes that the
nine declared classification functions are pairwise distinct.
It does not establish that their generated equivalence relations are
pairwise different on an eligible common domain, or that the respective
identity judgments are scientifically warranted.
The finite profile assignment of
Theorem~\ref{se200.thm.CoreRegimeAssignment} is likewise deterministic
within the selected catalog, not a derivation of which identity basis a
real-world referent must have.

Proposition~\ref{se200.prop.ClassificationDistinctionPersists}
establishes a narrower monotonicity statement: a classification
difference persists under extension of the transformation basis when
its earlier classifications are retained.
It says nothing about the minimum number of irreducible regimes.
Extending the basis may reveal additional candidate distinctions or
inadequacies in previously assigned classifications; such outcomes
require their own analysis.

The selected single-basis entries do not assert uniqueness.
Records may require distinctions among content, version, token,
signature, and chain of custody.
Occurrences may be analyzed as
bounded happenings, phases, or event complexes.
Obligation-bearing
parties may be distinguished from offices, roles, and organizational
units.
Whether any such distinction determines identity depends on
an independently justified criterion for the intended referent.

Section~\ref{sec:distinctions} also gives a carrier-indexed combination of
separate equivalence relations.
That construction shows why distinct
carrier kinds alone do not prove non-amalgamation.
It does not
establish that every identity account can be combined without loss.
A minimum-regime theorem would require a justified unit of counting,
adequate identity semantics, and a demonstrated obstruction to all
admissible combinations that purport to preserve the required judgments.
Whether a lossless union of the carrier-specific relations exists
remains an open test of that issue.

\subsection{Limits of the Selected Setting}
\label{subsec:limits_excluded_systems}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following cases expose questions that the selected construction
does not settle.
They should not be read as blanket exclusions of
otherwise legitimate representational methods.

\paragraph{Interpretively dependent identity.}
A framework may supply a causal, normative, or institutional criterion
under which a referent is identified.
Such a judgment can be recorded
as an attributed interpretation.
Treating it as an unqualified shared
referential commitment requires independent grounds and compatibility
with the applicable admissible frameworks.
The present profile catalog
does not establish those grounds.

\paragraph{Relation-supported identity.}
Relations may provide evidence or conditions for individuation,
co-reference, or persistence.
Their role cannot be ruled out by
stipulation.
When a construction uses relational criteria, it must
explain what they establish, for which referents, and under what
interpretation conditions.
This paper does not derive a general
prohibition on relation-determined identity.

\paragraph{Framework-relative identity judgments.}
Admissible frameworks may disagree about co-reference or persistence.
Recording their distinct judgments does not itself establish a common
identity judgment.
If the substrate adopts a contested judgment as
foundational, the Referential Common Ground premise must be
re-examined.
Disagreement alone, however, does not prove that all
shared reference has failed.

\paragraph{Declared parameters and undeclared distinctions.}
A role, flag, carrier tag, or identity-basis parameter is not
intrinsically a hidden regime.
An explicit parameter can preserve a
meaningful distinction within a combined representation.
Conversely, an undocumented discriminator or a parameter whose meaning
varies across participating frameworks may fail to support warranted
shared identity judgments.
Whether a proposed combination is faithful
must be assessed rather than decided by its number of labels.

\paragraph{Unmodeled transformations.}
The selected transformation basis $\Fset$ is not complete.
An application may involve operations whose effects differ from the
proposed family-level assignments or require finer typing.
Such cases limit the adequacy of the current catalog for that
application.
They do not by themselves establish additional irreducible
regimes or rescue the withdrawn lower-bound claim.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Non-Goals and Weakened Objectives}
\label{subsec:limits_nongoals}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

This paper does not construct causal or normative interpretive
frameworks, determine which interpretation should govern, or prescribe
a universal ontology.
It does not provide a method for discovering the
correct identity criterion for an arbitrary referent, certify the
soundness of transformation classifications in deployed systems, or
establish a general procedure for deciding strict identity.

The paper also does not prescribe that every identity-relevant
distinction must be implemented as a separate regime object.
A construction using explicit carrier or basis parameters may be
suitable if its semantics are fixed, its judgments are warranted,
and the required distinctions are preserved.
A context-relative construction may serve a different objective,
provided the limits on shared reference and interpretation are stated.
The scientific questions are what the construction preserves and why
those preservation claims are justified, not merely where a distinction
appears in a schema.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Boundary with Accountable Records}
\label{subsec:limits_boundary_accountable_records}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

This paper investigates candidate identity bases and their proposed
behavior under transformations of representations.
It does not provide a conformance checker for deployed record systems,
mandate a schema for basis declarations or transformation logs, or
establish that an implementation discloses every identity-relevant
mechanism and use.

Related operational-identity and accountable-records work can examine
whether implemented behavior agrees with a declared criterion and
whether relevant evidence is observable.
Such an audit cannot, solely from agreement with a declaration,
certify that the declaration supplies a scientifically adequate
identity criterion.
Conversely, the finite construction here does not
impose nine necessary implementation categories on such an audit.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Boundary of the Claim}
\label{subsec:limits_boundary_claim}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Within the specified finite construction, the nine named profiles
are pairwise distinct as classification functions, the selected
carrier--basis assignment is determinate, and existing classification
distinctions persist under extensions that retain the original
classifications.
The conditional transformation comparisons show how
different proposed identity bases can assign different effects to an
operation family.

These are limited formal results.
They do not establish that the
selected profiles yield nine distinct, scientifically warranted
identity relations or that every neutral accountability substrate
must realize nine irreducible regimes.
Establishing such a necessity claim would require additional,
independently justified conditions; the present paper leaves that
question open.
