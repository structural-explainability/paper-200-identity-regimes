\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern accountability systems increasingly operate under legal,
institutional, and analytic disagreement.
A shared record may need to
support reference to the same intended subject while its representations
are amended, forked, refined, decomposed, aggregated, or reclassified.
Agreement about what a record means cannot be assumed, and neither can
agreement about which identity conditions apply to its referents.
This paper investigates candidate ways to characterize individuation,
co-reference, and persistence under those transformations.

\emph{Neutral Substrates}~\citep{case2026neutral} provides the motivating
constraint: under Referential Common Ground and the stated foundational-layer
restriction, a substrate satisfies semantic neutrality relative to its
admissible frameworks.
That conditional result does not establish a
referential identity criterion for a particular bearer.
The present paper
examines what a declared criterion and selected transformation behavior
can express, while keeping the adequacy of those choices open to
independent justification.

The analysis pairs proposed identity bases with classifications of selected
transformation families.
An identity regime, in the terminology of this
paper, is a declared basis together with its transformation profile.
A classification records a proposed preservation, breakage, neutrality,
or inapplicability judgment; it does not independently prove that the
judgment describes identity in the world.
Likewise, the equivalence
relation generated from non-breaking steps is a formal construction,
not by itself a warrant for strict identity of the represented referent.

We select six reference kinds for analysis: obligation-bearers, rules,
occurrences, scopes, records, and plain referents.
The construction uses
nine candidate carrier--basis profiles.
Three pairs distinguish a locus
from an object occupying it, a scope's coverage from its internal
structure, and a rule's content from its structure.
Forking,
aggregation or decomposition, and refinement provide possible tests of
those distinctions.
Their proposed identity effects must be evaluated
against the intended referent, the eligible presentations, and an
independently justified identity criterion.

Within the selected construction, the nine classification functions are
pairwise distinct, and the specified carrier--basis assignment is
mathematically determinate.
These are limited results.
Different
classification profiles need not yield different generated equivalence
relations, and neither kind of formal distinction alone establishes
scientifically warranted strict identity.
Nor does the selection of six
carrier kinds and nine profiles prove a minimum number of irreducible
identity regimes.
A carrier-indexed combination can preserve separate
relations within one explicitly tagged relation, making the meaning of
``regime count'' and any proposed non-amalgamation requirement matters
for further investigation.

The contribution is therefore a finite comparative framework for
examining identity criteria under transformation, including formal
profile distinctions, conditional transformation witnesses, a
specified assignment, and a counterpoint to the earlier counting
argument.
It identifies open obligations rather than resolving them
by stipulation: independently grounded identity bases, sound
classification of transformation instances, the interpretation of
generated relations, and whether any faithful realization requires
irreducibly separate regimes.
The application settings are tests of
those questions, not validations of all selected identity criteria.

The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:background} states the Neutral Substrate foundation
and the narrower contribution pursued here.
Section~\ref{sec:formal_setting} sets out the formal setting and
transformation classifications.
Section~\ref{sec:inventory} specifies the selected reference inventory.
Section~\ref{sec:algebra} develops conditional comparisons among identity
bases, and Section~\ref{sec:nine} collects the nine candidate profiles.
Section~\ref{sec:bound} examines their distinctions and the limits of
regime counting.
Section~\ref{sec:determinacy} establishes determinacy of the selected
profile assignment, while Section~\ref{sec:application} treats three
application settings as identity-criterion tests.
Section~\ref{sec:related} situates the work in prior research;
Section~\ref{sec:limits} states its boundaries; and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
