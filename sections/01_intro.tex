\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern accountability systems increasingly operate under legal,
institutional, and analytic disagreement.
A shared record may need to
support reference to the same intended subject while its representations
are amended, forked, refined, decomposed, aggregated, or reclassified.
Agreement about what a record means cannot be assumed, and neither can
agreement about which identity conditions apply to its referents.
This paper investigates candidate ways to characterize individuation,
co-reference, and persistence under those transformations.

\emph{Neutral Substrates}~\citep{case2026neutral} provides the motivating
constraint: under Referential Common Ground and the stated foundational-layer
restriction, a substrate satisfies semantic neutrality relative to its
admissible frameworks.
That conditional result does not establish a
referential identity criterion for a particular bearer.
The present paper
examines what a declared criterion and selected transformation behavior
can express, while keeping the adequacy of those choices open to
independent justification.

The analysis pairs proposed identity bases with classifications of selected
transformation families.
An identity regime, in the terminology of this
paper, is a declared basis together with its transformation profile.
A classification records a proposed preservation, breakage, neutrality,
or inapplicability judgment relative to a declared basis.
Relations generated from admissible transformation steps are formal
constructions; their interpretation as strict identity requires
independent justification.

We select reference kinds for analysis: obligation-bearers, rules,
occurrences, scopes, records, and plain referents.
For each we propose candidate carrier--basis profiles.
Some kinds receive paired bases that distinguish a locus
from an object occupying it, a scope's coverage from its internal
structure, and a rule's content from its structure.
Forking, aggregation or decomposition, and refinement provide
tests of those distinctions.

The candidate profiles have pairwise distinct classification
functions, and the carrier--basis assignment is determinate.
Whether distinct profiles generate distinct equivalence relations
depends on the generating rule, the admissible transformation
dynamics, and the comparison domain (Appendix~\ref{app:sep}).
A carrier-indexed combination holds separate relations within one
explicitly tagged relation (Section~\ref{sec:distinctions}).

The principal contribution is a formal method for
deriving and examining identity-preservation classifications
under transformation.

First, identity claims become auditable: a verdict follows
from declared identity invariants, admissible transformation
steps, and explicit assumptions that can be independently
examined.

Second, the construction supplies testable conditions for
record-transforming systems.
An admissible transformation step that preserves
one declared invariant but changes another
provides a concrete witness of different generated
identity relations.

Third, the formalization identifies which assumptions
carry each result.
The scope and rule examples expose
field-granularity and transformation-domain dependencies;
the fork example exposes the need for explicit
continuity warrants.

The classifications, generated relations, and the distinctions among
them are formal results about the declared bases and models.
Applying them to a particular referent requires an independently
justified identity criterion (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:background} states the Neutral Substrate foundation
and the narrower contribution pursued here.
Section~\ref{sec:formal_setting} sets out the formal setting and
transformation classifications.
Section~\ref{sec:inventory} specifies the selected reference inventory.
Section~\ref{sec:algebra} develops conditional comparisons among identity
bases, and Section~\ref{sec:profiles} collects the candidate profiles.
Section~\ref{sec:distinctions} compares their generated relations and
shows how carrier-specific relations combine.
Section~\ref{sec:determinacy} establishes determinacy of the selected
profile assignment, while Section~\ref{sec:application} treats
application settings as identity-criterion tests.
Section~\ref{sec:related} situates the work in prior research;
Section~\ref{sec:limits} states its boundaries; and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
