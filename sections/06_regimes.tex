\section{Candidate Carrier--Basis Profiles}
\label{sec:profiles}
% =============================================================================

Sections~\ref{sec:inventory} and \ref{sec:algebra} yield a catalog of
named carrier--basis profiles, selected for analysis.

The following labels name the selected profiles:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2.5cm]
  \item[$\OBL$] obligation-bearer identity.
  \item[$\OCC$] occurrence identity.
  \item[$\REC$] record identity.
  \item[$\LOC$] locus-fixed plain-referent identity.
  \item[$\OBJ$] object-fixed plain-referent identity.
  \item[$\SCOPEE$] extension-fixed scope identity.
  \item[$\SCOPES$] structure-fixed scope identity.
  \item[$\RULEC$] content-fixed rule identity.
  \item[$\RULES$] structure-fixed rule identity.
\end{description}

Table~\ref{tab:master} records proposed classifications over the finite
transformation basis $\Fset$.
Its entries are stipulated family-level profile assignments for the
selected comparison settings.
They must not be read as established
identity effects of every operation in the named family or as
independently warranted judgments about real-world referents.
Where eligible operations within a family have different effects,
that family requires a more specific classification domain.

A \PRS{} entry proposes preservation of the declared identity basis;
a \BRK{} entry proposes its breakage.
An \NEU{} entry indicates an applicable operation that does not affect
the basis, and an \NA{} entry indicates inapplicability on the selected
domain.
Neither \PRS{} nor \NEU{} independently warrants strict identity
between actual referents.
In particular, a \BRK{} entry in a proposed profile does not by itself
establish that two specified objects are distinct.

For the selected same-carrier comparisons, BF distinguishes the
proposed $\LOC$ and $\OBJ$ classifications; AD distinguishes
$\SCOPEE$ and $\SCOPES$; and RF distinguishes $\RULEC$
and $\RULES$.
The conditional examples in Section~\ref{sec:algebra} show how
stipulated locus, object, extension, structure, and content criteria
can yield these classification differences.
The other entries record additional proposed behavior whose adequacy
also requires examination; they are not needed to establish the
conditional split comparisons.

The uniform RE and SU rows are features of this selected table:
RE is assigned \NEU{} throughout, while SU is assigned \BRK{} throughout.
Those assignments are not general theorems about re-expression or
substitution under every scientifically adequate identity criterion.

A disagreement between two classification functions is distinct from
a disagreement between the equivalence relations generated by
non-breaking steps.
To state the latter, the two relations must be
compared on a common eligible domain.

\begin{definition}[Separation witness]
  \label{se200.def.SeparationWitness}
  Let $\tau$ and $\tau'$ be profiles with a common domain $D$ of
  eligible presentations.
  A \emph{separation witness for their generated
    relations on $D$} is a pair $(r,s)\in D\times D$ for which
  \[
    r\sim_\tau s \quad\text{and}\quad r\not\sim_{\tau'}s,
  \]
  or conversely, with each relation restricted to $D$.
\end{definition}

Such a witness demonstrates that the generated relations differ on
$D$; it does not independently establish the correctness of either
relation as strict real-world identity.
Nor does a difference in the
classification of a transformation family necessarily provide such
a witness, since equivalence closure may connect the relevant pair
by another non-breaking path.

\begin{proposition}[Distinct core profiles]
  \label{se200.prop.DistinctCoreProfiles}
  The nine classification functions specified by the columns of
  Table~\ref{tab:master} are pairwise distinct.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  It is enough to inspect the entries at AN, RF, RA, and BF.
  The four-entry classification signatures in these rows are
  pairwise different across all columns of
  Table~\ref{tab:master}.
  Hence no two of the
  classification functions agree on all of $\Fset$.
\end{proof}

This proposition concerns the \emph{declared classification functions}.
Whether their generated relations are pairwise distinct on a common
domain is taken up in Section~\ref{sec:distinctions} and
Appendix~\ref{app:sep}.

\subsection{Initial Model-Derived Classification Results}
\label{subsec:model_derived_results}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Table~\ref{tab:master} records the proposed classification matrix.
Its entries are not inputs to the following derivations.

Instead, the companion Lean models~\citep{case2026identityregimes}
specify presentation fields, identity invariants,
admissible transformation steps, and their effects.

An invariant basis consists of a function on presentations
and a declared support of presentation fields.
Agreement on the supported fields implies equality of the invariant.
The Lean development additionally proves minimality of the
supports used in the certified cells: every supported field
can independently affect its invariant.

An effect model declares admissible transformation steps and
the fields each transformation family may write.
An effective instance is an admissible step that changes
at least one presentation field.

\begin{definition}[Model-derived transformation verdict]
  \label{se200.def.ModelDerivedVerdict}
  Fix an invariant basis $b$, an effect model $M$, and a
  transformation family $f$.

  The derived verdict is:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),nosep]
    \item $\NA$: no effective instance exists;
    \item $\BRK$: effective instances exist and every one
          changes the invariant;
    \item $\MIX$: some effective instance changes the invariant
          and another preserves it;
    \item $\PRS$: effective instances exist, all preserve the
          invariant, and at least one changes a supported field;
    \item $\NEU$: effective instances exist, all preserve the
          invariant, and none changes a supported field.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\MIX{} establishes that the declared transformation family contains
both invariant-preserving and invariant-breaking effective instances.

The family therefore cannot receive a uniform preserving or breaking
verdict under those modeled conditions.
A uniform verdict would require restricting or refining the family.

Alternatively, individual preservation judgments can be retained
at the step level without assigning one uniform verdict to the family.

Applicability is defined for the model's presentation space,
not for an arbitrarily selected smaller comparison domain.

Each certified cell contains an explicit effect model,
an invariant basis with a proven minimal support,
and a proof of its derived verdict.
The remaining cells in the stipulated matrix
have no derived model yet.
For every certified cell, the derived and stipulated
classifications agree on whether the transformation is breaking.

The derived \NEU{} cells are classified \PRS{}
in Table~\ref{tab:master}.
Both verdicts are non-breaking, but they distinguish
whether a transformation engages a supported field.

The scope and rule comparisons expose dependencies on
field granularity.
The fork classification additionally depends on explicit
continuity warrants.
These conditional results do not establish a universal
correspondence between stipulated and derived classifications.

The additional rows in Table~\ref{tab:derived_initial}
are sensitivity analyses or alternative warranted models,
not additional certified matrix cells.

Two findings are especially consequential.
First, refining the scope presentation from one organization field
to one field per case-pair changes the strict AD extension verdict
from \PRS{} to \NEU{}.
The fine-grained extension basis reads diagonal entries (coverage),
while restructuring changes only off-diagonal entries.
The support of each fine-grained scope basis is proved minimal in that
model.
Both verdicts are non-breaking, but the classification is
not invariant under the choice of field representation.
The model therefore exposes a granularity dependence that must be
controlled before an unqualified classification can be asserted.

The rule model exhibits a corresponding dependence.
Under strict RF, the content basis is \PRS{} when the organization
is represented coarsely, but \NEU{} when fields separately
represent case-pair organization and case-specific norms.
The structure basis is \BRK{} in both representations.
The field-level content support reads diagonal organization
entries and the case-specific norms; strict RF preserves both.
The content and structure supports are proved minimal in this model.

Second, branching alone specifies a change of branch, not a change of
locus or object.
With no additional warrant, both corresponding verdicts are \MIX.
Under the Colocated and FreshObject warrants,
the locus basis is neutral and the object basis breaking;
under the Colocated and SameObject warrants,
both are neutral and their step-generated relations
collapse on every comparison domain.
These outcomes are consequences of distinct, explicitly stated
hypotheses.
None establishes that one warrant is true of every fork
in an actual system.

The broader scope variant in the table permits changes to coverage and
therefore is not an instance of the coverage-preserving AD family as
originally defined in Section~\ref{sec:formal_setting}.
It is included as a separate sensitivity analysis.
The broader rule variant likewise changes the specification of eligible refinements.
A comparison with Table~\ref{tab:master} is warranted only after
matching operation semantics, identity basis, field granularity, and
admissibility conditions.

The invariant-based step construction uses a presentation space $C$,
an effect model $M$, an invariant basis $b$, and a comparison domain
$D\subseteq C$.

Define the preserving-step relation by
\[
  R_{M,b,D}(x,y)
  \iff
  x,y\in D
  \;\land\;
  \exists f\,
  \bigl(
  M.\mathrm{step}(f,x,y)
  \land
  b.\mathrm{inv}(x)=b.\mathrm{inv}(y)
  \bigr).
\]

The step-generated identity relation is
\[
  \mathrm{StepId}_{M,b,D}
  =
  \operatorname{EqCl}_{C}(R_{M,b,D}),
\]
where $\operatorname{EqCl}_{C}$ is the smallest equivalence
relation on $C$ containing the specified generating steps.

Unlike the classification-based family-level constructions,
this relation tests invariant preservation for each admissible
step. Its generating edges may include preserving steps from
families whose derived verdict is \MIX{}.

\begin{theorem}[Step-generated relation equality]
  \label{se200.thm.StepIdentityCollapse}
  Fix a common effect model, two invariant bases $b$ and $b'$,
  and a comparison domain $D$.

  The equivalence relations generated by their respective
  invariant-preserving admissible steps are equal if and only if
  the bases agree on invariant preservation for every admissible
  step with both endpoints in $D$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  If preservation judgments agree on every admissible step
  in $D$, the generating relations are equal.
  Their equivalence closures therefore agree.

  Conversely, suppose the generated relations agree.
  An admissible step preserving $b$ belongs to the relation
  generated by $b$ and hence also to the relation generated
  by $b'$.

  Every generating step for the latter relation preserves
  $b'$. Equality of invariant values is an equivalence
  relation, so its closure also preserves $b'$.
  Thus the original step preserves $b'$.
  The converse follows symmetrically.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Separating transformation step]
  \label{se200.prop.StepSeparation}
  Let $x,y\in D$ be connected by an admissible
  transformation step.

  If $b(x)=b(y)$ but $b'(x)\ne b'(y)$, then the
  step-generated identity relations for $b$ and
  $b'$ are distinct.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The step generates a relation between $x$ and $y$
  under $b$.

  Under $b'$, the generated relation cannot connect
  $x$ and $y$, because every generating step preserves
  $b'$, and equality of invariant values is preserved
  under equivalence closure.
\end{proof}

These derivations are mathematically conditional.

Independently justifying the invariants and fork warrants for actual referents,
and showing that a generated equivalence relation expresses warranted
strict identity, remain separate scientific obligations.
